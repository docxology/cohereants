# Symbols and Glossary {#sec:symbols_glossary}

## Key Terms and Definitions

### Olfaction and Chemosensation
- **Olfaction**: The sense of smell; the ability to detect and identify airborne molecules
- **Chemosensation**: The detection of chemical stimuli by sensory cells
- **Semiochemicals**: Chemical substances that carry information between organisms
- **Pheromones**: Semiochemicals that affect the behavior of other members of the same species
- **Cuticular Hydrocarbons (CHCs)**: Long-chain hydrocarbons found on the surface of insects that serve as recognition cues

### Insect Anatomy
- **Antennae**: Paired sensory appendages on the head of insects
- **Sensilla**: Microscopic sensory hairs or pegs on insect antennae and other body parts
- **Sensilla Trichodea**: Hair-like sensilla that are often involved in olfaction
- **Sensilla Basiconica**: Peg-like sensilla with porous surfaces
- **Sensilla Coeloconica**: Pit-like sensilla that may detect temperature and humidity
- **ORN**: Olfactory Receptor Neuron; nerve cells that respond to chemical stimuli

### Electromagnetic Theory
- **Infrared (IR)**: Electromagnetic radiation with wavelengths longer than visible light (0.7-1000 μm)
- **Mid-infrared**: IR radiation in the 2-25 μm range
- **Far-infrared**: IR radiation in the 25-1000 μm range
- **Dielectric**: A material that can be polarized by an electric field
- **Waveguide**: A structure that guides electromagnetic waves along a specific path
- **Resonator**: A device or structure that oscillates at specific frequencies

### Spectroscopy and Molecular Properties
- **Vibrational Theory**: The hypothesis that olfaction works by detecting molecular vibrations
- **Emission Spectrum**: The range of wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation emitted by a substance
- **Transmission Window**: A range of wavelengths where the atmosphere is relatively transparent
- **Deuteration**: The replacement of hydrogen atoms with deuterium (heavy hydrogen) in molecules
- **Enantiomers**: Mirror-image forms of the same molecule
- **FRET**: Förster Resonance Energy Transfer; energy transfer between molecules

## Mathematical Notation

### Wavelength and Frequency
- **λ (lambda)**: Wavelength of electromagnetic radiation
- **ν (nu)**: Frequency of electromagnetic radiation
- **c**: Speed of light (3 × 10^8 m/s)
- **μm**: Micrometer (10^-6 meters)
- **cm^-1**: Wavenumber (reciprocal wavelength)

### Physical Constants
- **h**: Planck's constant (6.626 × 10^-34 J·s)
- **k**: Boltzmann constant (1.381 × 10^-23 J/K)
- **T**: Temperature in Kelvin

### Insect Measurements
- **μm**: Micrometer; typical size range for insect sensilla
- **nm**: Nanometer; scale of molecular interactions
- **ms**: Millisecond; typical response time of insect ORNs

## Abbreviations

- **OR**: Olfactory Receptor
- **ORNs**: Olfactory Receptor Neurons
- **CHCs**: Cuticular Hydrocarbons
- **IR**: Infrared
- **FIR**: Far Infrared
- **MIR**: Mid Infrared
- **NIR**: Near Infrared
- **ATR-FTIR**: Attenuated Total Reflectance Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy
- **FRET**: Förster Resonance Energy Transfer
- **GPCR**: G-Protein Coupled Receptor
- **MTs**: Microtubules

## Key Concepts

### Atmospheric Transmission Windows
The Earth's atmosphere has specific wavelength ranges where infrared radiation can travel relatively freely:
- **2-5 μm**: Mid-infrared window
- **8-14 μm**: Long-wave infrared window
- **17-25 μm**: Far-infrared window

### Sensilla Dimensions and Wavelength Matching
Insect sensilla have dimensions that correspond to the wavelengths of infrared radiation:
- **6-160 μm**: Typical range of sensilla trichodea lengths
- **2-8 μm**: Typical range of sensilla basiconica lengths
- **1-30 μm**: Wavelength range for optimal infrared detection

### Response Time Comparisons
- **Insect ORNs**: 1-5 ms response time
- **Insect Photoreceptors**: 0.1 ms response time
- **Insect Auditory Receptors**: 0.16 ms response time
- **Traditional Olfaction**: 7-12 ms response time

## Research Methodology Terms

- **Ionotropic**: Direct ligand-gated ion channels
- **Metabotropic**: G-protein coupled receptor systems
- **Piezoelectric**: Materials that generate electric charge in response to mechanical stress
- **Allosteric Modulation**: Changes in protein function due to binding at sites other than the active site
- **Photomodulation**: Changes in protein function due to light absorption
