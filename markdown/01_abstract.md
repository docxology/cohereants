# Abstract {#sec:abstract}

**Objective:** Assess whether insects detect infrared (IR) vibrational signatures of semiochemicals and produce falsifiable, quantitative predictions by integrating morphology, spectroscopy, neural data, and deterministic computational models.

**Methods:** We combine: (i) morphological analysis of sensilla across taxa (N>500), (ii) ATR‑FTIR spectroscopy of CHCs (±0.1 μm peak resolution), (iii) a meta‑analysis of ORN latencies, and (iv) deterministic, unit‑tested computational models. All analyses are reproducible (seed = 42).

**Results:** Sensilla dimensions correlate with predicted IR resonances (r ≥ 0.85). Modeled ORN latencies (1–5 ms) are 2.3–7× faster than diffusion‑limited estimates. CHC spectra overlap atmospheric transmission windows (~89%), implying detection ranges of 10–100 m. Integrated case studies reproduce theoretical predictions within ~15%. See \Cref{fig:response_time_comparison,fig:atmospheric_transmission,fig:chc_spectra_example}.

**Conclusions:** The framework yields three concrete, testable predictions: sensilla wavelength tuning, IR‑only behavioral responses, and sub‑10 ms neural responses to electromagnetic stimulation. We provide minimal falsifiers that distinguish electromagnetic effects from thermal confounds. Applications include biomimetic IR sensors and targeted pest management.

Reproducibility: Seven case studies and full computational details are in the Appendices (\cref{sec:app_sensilla_array,sec:app_environmental_channel,sec:app_detection_limits,sec:app_neural_encoding,sec:app_spectral_unmixing,sec:app_plasmonic_geometry,sec:app_active_inference}) and the mathematical appendix (\cref{sec:mathematical_appendix}).
