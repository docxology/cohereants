# Abstract {#sec:abstract}

Olfaction, vision, and other sensory modalities interact to shape behavior and learning across the natural world, particularly in insects, and in this paper, we review several disparate bodies of empirical entomological evidence with an open eye and mind to the effects of visual and non-visual photonic spectra, paying specific attention to the interesting convergence of evidence from multiple domains upon the infra-red wavelength range; primarily, we consider the phenomena of vision and chemosensation (olfaction) and explore how a broader understanding of the biological relevance of infra-red light may lead to unique explanations, predictions, and experiments, suggesting that including the possible mechanism of infra-red influence on olfaction could augment traditional semiochemical receptor-based accounts by drawing on models of insect antennal sensilla as dielectric waveguides and a resonating mixed-phase meta-material; within entomology, we speculate on the role of infra-red light in various cognitive and behavioral phenomena, such as nestmate recognition in eusocial Hymenoptera, sexual and trail pheromone detection, necrophoresis, and parasite-host interactions, while also briefly sketching some implications of a fuller understanding of infra-red light's entomological function across domains such as cognitive-behavioral, agronomical, evolutionary-ecological, and collective intelligence.
